TrueNAS ZFS Storage Architecture: Vdev Layout, Capacity and Failure Domains

Build predictable TrueNAS storage by aligning ZFS vdev design, capacity planning, protection level and workload behaviour.

Published August 25, 2026 · RCW IT Training

Core design principles

1. Choose vdev layout first.

Choose vdev layout first. Mirrors favour random I/O and predictable latency; RAIDZ layouts favour capacity efficiency but have different performance and expansion characteristics.

2. Avoid running pools near capacity.

Avoid running pools near capacity. Snapshot growth, copy-on-write behaviour and resilver operations need headroom; establish alerts and a capacity threshold before production use.

3. Separate workload classes when practical.

Separate workload classes when practical. Virtual machines, backup targets and sequential archive data have different I/O patterns and should not compete blindly.

Operational checklist

Define ownership, document the architecture and dependencies, protect privileged access, monitor the service, test recovery or rollback, and review the design after every material change.

Key takeaway: Document disks, vdevs, pool purpose, encryption-key custody, snapshot policy and recovery steps. Storage design is an operational contract, not just a capacity number.
