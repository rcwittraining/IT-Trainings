About RCW IT Training

RCW IT Training is a free, browser-based practice ground for people who run — or want to run — enterprise infrastructure: Linux and RHEL, Microsoft Intune and Azure, AWS and GCP, VMware, storage, backup and networking. Everything on the site is public, needs no account, and is written by one person who still does this work every day.

Pradeep Raju

Enterprise Infrastructure Architect · Chennai, India · 19+ years in production environments

I design and operate infrastructure for large organisations — VMware and Linux estates, Active Directory and Entra ID, Microsoft Intune device management, backup and storage platforms, and workloads on AWS, Azure and GCP. RCW IT Training started as the practice environment I wished I had when I was preparing for my first certifications, and grew into the labs, simulators and troubleshooting notes published here.

How the content is made — and what it is not

Every lab and guide begins with a real scenario from production work or from a certification objective. I build and test it in a lab environment (RHEL 10, vSphere, an Intune tenant, cloud sandboxes), write it up, and publish it here. Where I use AI tools, it is for proof-reading and formatting; the technical content, command sequences and the mistakes described come from operating these systems. If a page reads like a vendor manual, that is a bug — please tell me.

The site is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by Red Hat, Microsoft, VMware/Broadcom, Cisco, Citrix, Amazon, Google or any other vendor named here. Practice questions are original and are not exam dumps; RHCSA tasks are original exercises aligned to public objectives, not exam content. All product names and trademarks belong to their owners.

Corrections and update policy

  • Every article shows a published date and, when changed, an updated date.
  • Guides are re-checked when a product version changes the behaviour described (for example, RHEL point releases and Intune service updates).
  • Reported errors are fixed within a week and acknowledged on the page with credit to the reporter, unless they prefer otherwise.

Privacy, advertising and how the site stays free

Labs run entirely in your browser; names entered for certificates never leave your device. Some informational pages show Google AdSense advertising, which pays for hosting and domain costs. Ads are never placed inside labs, exams or scored challenges. You can change your consent at any time from the Privacy and cookie settings link in the footer.
